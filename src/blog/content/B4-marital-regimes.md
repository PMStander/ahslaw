# Community of Property vs Accrual: Which Marital Regime Actually Protects You?

Most South African couples walk out of Home Affairs as newlyweds without really understanding the financial rules they've just signed up for. They only discover what marital regime they're in years later — usually when debts pile up or divorce becomes reality. By then it's often too late to fix easily.

That single choice (or the default the law chooses for you) decides whose debts you're stuck with, whether the business you built stays yours, and how everything gets divided if the marriage ends.

This plain-English guide explains the three options so you can make a smart decision before you marry — or understand exactly where you stand if you're already married.

## What is a Marital Regime?

A marital regime is the legal rulebook that governs how a couple's assets, debts, and finances are owned and divided — both while married and when the marriage ends through divorce or death.

In South Africa you have three main choices. You must decide **before** the wedding. If you don't sign an antenuptial contract (ANC) and register it, the law automatically puts you in community of property.

## The Three Regimes at a Glance

| Regime | Assets & Growth | Debts & Risk | Best Suited For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In Community of Property** (Default) | Everything merges into one joint estate — 50/50 ownership | Both spouses are fully liable for all debts | Couples with very similar finances and low risk |
| **Out of Community without Accrual** | Complete separation — nothing is shared | Each spouse responsible for their own debts only | Second marriages, large pre-marital estates, or couples wanting total independence |
| **Out of Community with Accrual** | Assets stay separate during marriage, but growth (accrual) is shared equally at the end | Each spouse responsible for their own debts only | Most modern couples — fairness without losing control |

**Note:** Anything other than community of property requires a properly drafted and registered antenuptial contract before the wedding.

## The Real Risks of "In Community of Property"

This is the default regime, and many people still believe it's the "fair" or "romantic" choice. In practice, it can be the riskiest.

From the day you marry, your separate financial lives end. All assets and debts — before and during the marriage — become one joint estate. You each own 50%. You are also **jointly and severally liable** for every debt.

That means:

- Your spouse's secret credit-card spending
- A business loan that goes bad
- Even debts they brought into the marriage

If your spouse is sued or declared insolvent, the entire joint estate (including your half) can be affected. Your house, savings, and credit record are all on the line. This matters especially where debt is already a concern: [in South Africa, civil debt carries serious consequences](/blog/you-cannot-be-arrested-for-debt), and in community of property those consequences can affect both spouses.

## Why Most Couples Prefer "Out of Community with Accrual"

This is the regime most modern couples actually want once they understand the options.

**How it works:**

- During the marriage you each keep your own assets and handle your own debts.
- In the antenuptial contract you declare the starting value of your estates (called the commencement value).
- When the marriage ends, you calculate the **growth** (accrual) of each estate, adjusted for inflation.
- The spouse whose estate grew less gets half the difference from the other.

**Simple example:**

You start with R300 000. Your spouse starts with R50 000.

Twenty years later you have R4 million and your spouse has R800 000.

Your accrual: R3.7 million. Theirs: R750 000.

Difference: R2.95 million → Your spouse is entitled to R1.475 million (half the difference).

You share the growth of the marriage without sharing the risk. Inheritances, gifts, and certain personal injury damages are normally excluded from the accrual unless your ANC says otherwise.

## Out of Community without Accrual

This is total separation. Your assets and debts stay completely yours. There is no sharing of growth at the end. It is usually chosen in second marriages or where one partner has significantly more assets and wants full protection, but it can leave the lower-earning spouse feeling short-changed.

## How to Check Which Regime You're In

- Look at your marriage certificate — it will mention an ANC if one was registered.
- Search the Deeds Office for any registered antenuptial contract.
- Contact the attorney or notary who helped with your wedding.
- Visit your local Department of Home Affairs.

If you never signed anything before the wedding, you are almost certainly married **in community of property**.

## Can You Change Your Regime After Marriage?

Yes — but it takes a High Court application.

Under **Section 21 of the Matrimonial Property Act 88 of 1984**, both spouses can jointly ask the court to change their marital regime. The court will approve only if:

- There are sound reasons for the change,
- All creditors have been properly notified, and
- No one will be unfairly prejudiced.

The process usually takes 3-6 months and involves legal costs, but for couples stuck in a risky community-of-property marriage, it is often worth every cent.

## What Happens in a Divorce?

South Africa recognises only one ground for divorce: the irretrievable breakdown of the marriage (Divorce Act 70 of 1979).

How your assets are divided depends entirely on your regime:

- **Community of Property** → 50/50 split of the entire joint estate (assets and debts).
- **Out of Community without Accrual** → Each keeps what is in their own name.
- **Out of Community with Accrual** → Only the growth is shared — usually the fairest outcome.

Custody, contact, and maintenance are governed separately from the marital regime. If maintenance becomes a dispute after divorce, [this step-by-step guide covers how to enforce a maintenance order](/blog/maintenance-unpaid-what-to-do-next).

## Common Misconceptions

- **"In community of property protects me."**

It usually does the opposite — you become fully liable for your spouse's debts.

- **"I'm automatically entitled to half of everything."**

Only true in community of property. With accrual you share the growth, not the total assets.

- **"We don't need lawyers if we agree on everything."**

Even an uncontested divorce still needs a court order. Proper legal agreements protect both of you.

- **"Accrual is too complicated."**

Once set up correctly in your ANC, it is actually quite straightforward.
