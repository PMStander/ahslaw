# Unfair Dismissal and the CCMA Process Explained Simply

Losing a job is stressful enough. When you believe the dismissal was unfair, the situation becomes even more difficult. South African law gives employees clear protection against unfair dismissal, and the Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration (CCMA) is the main place where most of these disputes are resolved.

This article explains, in plain language, what counts as an unfair dismissal and how the CCMA process works.

## What is an unfair dismissal?

The Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 gives every employee the right not to be unfairly dismissed.

A dismissal is unfair if the employer cannot prove two things:

1.  There was a fair reason for the dismissal, and
2.  A fair procedure was followed.

Fair reasons generally fall into three categories:

-   The employee’s conduct (for example, serious misconduct)
-   The employee’s capacity (inability to do the job properly, including poor performance or ill health)
-   The employer’s operational requirements (retrenchment for genuine business reasons)

Both the reason and the process must be fair. Even if the employer had a good reason, a completely unfair procedure can still make the dismissal unfair.

There is also a more serious category called **automatically unfair dismissals**. These include dismissals related to pregnancy, unfair discrimination, participation in a protected strike, whistleblowing under the Protected Disclosures Act, or exercising rights under the Labour Relations Act. These attract higher potential compensation (up to 24 months’ remuneration) and often end up in the Labour Court.

## The Code of Good Practice on Dismissal

In September 2025 a new Code of Good Practice: Dismissal came into effect. It replaced the old Schedule 8. The core principles remain the same: employers must have a fair reason and follow a fair process. The new Code places more emphasis on genuine dialogue rather than overly formal, court-like disciplinary hearings, and it recognises that small businesses may not be able to follow the same elaborate procedures as large companies.

## The CCMA process – step by step

1.  **Refer the dispute quickly**
    For ordinary unfair dismissal claims you must refer the matter to the CCMA (or the relevant bargaining council) within **30 days** of the date of dismissal.

    Use Form LRA 7.11. You can download it from the CCMA website (ccma.org.za), complete it online, or get a copy at a CCMA office. There is no filing fee.

    If you miss the 30-day deadline you can apply for condonation (permission to refer late), but you must explain the delay properly. Condonation is not automatic.

2.  **Conciliation**
    The CCMA will set a conciliation meeting. A commissioner tries to help both sides reach a settlement. This is an informal process. You do not need a lawyer at this stage, and in many cases legal representation is limited.

    If the parties settle, the matter ends. If not, the commissioner issues a certificate of non-resolution.

3.  **Arbitration (or Labour Court)**
    If the dispute remains unresolved, you can take it further:

    -   Most ordinary unfair dismissal cases go to CCMA arbitration (you usually request this on Form LRA 7.13 within the required time after the certificate is issued).
    -   Automatically unfair dismissals and certain other matters are referred to the Labour Court.

    At arbitration the commissioner listens to both sides and makes a binding decision.

## What remedies are available?

If the dismissal is found to be unfair, the possible outcomes include:

-   **Reinstatement** (returning to the same job) – this is the preferred remedy
-   **Re-employment** in a similar position
-   **Compensation** (usually limited to a maximum of 12 months’ remuneration for ordinary unfair dismissals, and up to 24 months for automatically unfair dismissals)

The exact amount of compensation depends on factors such as how unfair the dismissal was, length of service, and efforts to find new work.

### Practical tips

-   Keep all documents: dismissal letter, warnings, emails, performance reviews, and any notes of meetings.
-   Ask the employer in writing for the reasons for the dismissal if they have not been given clearly.
-   Do not delay the CCMA referral while trying to negotiate with the employer. You can still talk while the referral is running.
-   Be honest and consistent in your version of events.

### Final note

Not every dismissal that feels unfair will succeed at the CCMA. The employer only needs to show that the reason was fair and that a reasonable procedure was followed, judged against the circumstances of the workplace. At the same time, many employees succeed when the process was clearly defective or the reason was weak.

If you have been dismissed and are unsure about your rights or the next steps, getting early advice can make a real difference — especially with the strict 30-day time limit.
