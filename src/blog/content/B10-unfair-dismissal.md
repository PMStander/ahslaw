# Unfair Dismissal and the CCMA Process Explained Simply

Losing a job is stressful enough. When you believe the dismissal was unfair, the situation becomes even more difficult. Fortunately, South African law provides a clear process for employees to challenge dismissals they believe are unfair. This process is handled by the Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration (CCMA).

## What is an unfair dismissal?

The Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 (LRA) recognizes three main grounds for fair dismissal:

1.  **Misconduct:** The employee has broken a workplace rule.
2.  **Incapacity:** The employee is unable to do the job due to poor performance or ill health.
3.  **Operational Requirements (Retrenchment):** The employer needs to downsize for economic, technological, or structural reasons.

A dismissal is unfair if the employer does not have a fair reason for the dismissal or if the employer did not follow a fair procedure.

### Fair Reason

The reason for the dismissal must be valid and fair. For example, an employer cannot dismiss an employee for a protected reason, such as pregnancy, trade union membership, or for making a protected disclosure (whistleblowing).

### Fair Procedure

Even if there is a fair reason for dismissal, the employer must still follow a fair procedure. This generally means:

*   Investigating the matter properly.
*   Giving the employee an opportunity to state their case and be represented.
*   Informing the employee of the decision and the reasons for it.

## The CCMA process

If you believe you have been unfairly dismissed, you can refer the dispute to the CCMA. Here’s a step-by-step guide to the process:

### Step 1: Referral to the CCMA

You must refer the dispute to the CCMA within 30 days of the date of dismissal. This is done by completing a CCMA referral form (LRA Form 7.11) and delivering a copy to your former employer and the CCMA.

### Step 2: Conciliation

The first step in the CCMA process is conciliation. This is an informal meeting where a CCMA commissioner tries to help you and your employer reach a settlement. The commissioner cannot force either party to agree to a settlement.

If a settlement is reached, it is recorded in writing and becomes a legally binding agreement.

### Step 3: Arbitration

If the dispute is not resolved at conciliation, it will be referred to arbitration. This is a more formal process, similar to a court hearing.

At arbitration, both you and your employer will have the opportunity to present evidence and call witnesses. The CCMA commissioner will then make a final and binding decision, called an arbitration award.

The commissioner can order the employer to:

*   Reinstate you (give you your job back).
*   Re-employ you (give you another job at the same company).
*   Pay you compensation.

## Automatically unfair dismissals

Some dismissals are considered "automatically unfair." These are dismissals for reasons that are contrary to public policy or infringe on an employee's fundamental rights. Examples of automatically unfair dismissals include dismissals for:

*   Participating in a protected strike.
*   Refusing to do the work of an employee who is on a protected strike.
*   Pregnancy or any reason related to pregnancy.
*   Making a protected disclosure (whistleblowing).
*   Discrimination on grounds such as race, gender, religion, or sexual orientation.

If you believe you have been automatically unfairly dismissed, you can refer the dispute to the Labour Court.
